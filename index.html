In development Independent AI project, 2026

GetMeAJob: An AI job-application engine that won't make things up

Generic AI tools can write applications that sound convincing but invent experience. GetMeAJob is built around one rule: every claim must trace back to real evidence. It turns several resumes into one profile, researches each employer, and will say when a role isn't worth applying for.

I defined the product and its rules and directed the build. Claude designed the architecture and wrote the code.

Internship Data Science Intern, Dublin, March–July 2024

Harris Group: Turning automotive data into business insight

I built Power BI dashboards and analyzed automotive inventory, sales and vehicle movement data across regions, then presented the findings to the Managing Director, executives and department leaders.

Sales and inventory data was spread across several reporting sources, so I recommended consolidating them into a single star-schema data model. The recommendation was approved, the sources were merged, and the dashboards went on to be used in leadership reviews.

Read the case study

The business questions I worked on

  • Dead stock. Which automotive parts had no recorded sales for 180 days or more, and needed management attention?
  • Regional allocation. How did demand and stock compare across regions, including for electric and diesel vehicles?
  • Replenishment. What do historical usage and supplier lead times suggest for safety-stock levels?
  • Data foundations. How could fragmented reporting sources become one consistent model?

Original dashboards and company data are confidential and aren't shown.

Completed Final-year research, University College Dublin, 2025

UCD Research: Does giving people control change how they feel about a platform?

For my final-year research, I built two versions of a short-form video app. One gave people visible controls and explained why each video was recommended; the other played videos endlessly with no explanation.

  • 47 participants completed the study.
  • Participants using the high-control interface reported a significantly greater sense of agency.
  • No statistically significant difference in clicking behavior was detected between the two groups.

The takeaway for product teams: usage numbers alone can miss how people actually feel about a product.
